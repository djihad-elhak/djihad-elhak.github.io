ViT-RealWaste and TrashGo

A leakage-aware benchmark of a Vision Transformer against three CNNs for landfill waste classification, and the Flutter app that runs the classifier on the phone and turns a correct photo into a reward.

Context
Personal project
Period
2026
Role
Benchmark, export and app
Stack

01

The question and the product

One project in two parts. ViT-RealWaste is the research question: under one identical, leakage-aware protocol, does a Vision Transformer beat representative CNNs on real landfill waste photos, and by how much compared with sampling noise? TrashGo is the product: a Flutter app that runs an ONNX export of the pipeline entirely on the phone and turns a correct classification into a claim, coins and missions.

The data is RealWaste (Single, Iranmanesh and Raad, University of Wollongong, 2023): 4,752 photos from a residential waste audit, in nine classes from cardboard to vegetation.

ViT-B/16 has the best macro-F1 and ties Inception V3 on accuracy, but their intervals overlap almost completely: 481 images cannot rank them.

The same errors (miscellaneous trash, metal and plastic) recur in every model, which points to label ambiguity rather than architecture as the main limit. The write-up is a manuscript, ViT-RealWaste: A Leakage-Aware Benchmark of a Vision Transformer against CNNs for Landfill Waste Classification.

Limits

  • One random split and one seed per model; the intervals ignore training variance.
  • No group-aware split, so photos of the same item can land in different partitions.
  • The validation set contains offline-augmented copies.

03

TrashGo, the app

  • Stack: Flutter and Dart, Riverpod, GoRouter, Firebase Auth and Firestore, onnxruntime_v2 on the CPU, flutter_map with OpenStreetMap, Cloudinary for photos.
  • One contract between notebook and app: NCHW float32 input, resize 256 then centre crop 224, ImageNet mean and standard deviation, the training class order, softmax before any threshold. If one line changes, the others change with it.
  • Shipped model: the app currently ships the DenseNet121 export, the checkpoint the notebook validated end to end in ONNX Runtime; it is not one of the four models in the comparison. Target: under 800 ms per inference on a mid-range Android phone.
  • Rewards: base coins grow with how rare a category is in the audit mix, with bonuses for the first claim of the day and for 3- and 7-day streaks.
