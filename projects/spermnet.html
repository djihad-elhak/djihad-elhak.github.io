SpermNet and the Andrology Console

Six months at CERIST building automated sperm motility analysis that runs on a laboratory PC without a GPU: a 0.38 M-parameter detector, a tracker with no appearance model, kinematic conventions that are stated and measured, and the desktop application around them.

Context
Research internship, CERIST
Period

Role
Designed, built and evaluated the system; wrote the paper
Stack
PyTorch, OpenCV, ONNX Runtime, NumPy, FastAPI, Electron, React, TypeScript

01

The problem

Motility is one of the first things read in a semen examination. To measure it from video, every spermatozoon has to be found, followed from frame to frame, turned into physical velocities and given a motility grade. Each of those steps is a computer vision problem with unusual numbers.

  • A head covers a median of 93.6 px², about 10 pixels across: 99.7% of detections are "small" in the COCO sense.
  • A frame holds from about 100 heads in a weak sample to more than 1,500 in a dense strong one, at 25 frames per second, with low contrast.
  • The number a clinician reads, the motile fraction, is a ratio. A false detection that does not move counts as an immotile cell, so the errors that matter are not the ones F1 weighs most.
  • The laboratory works on ordinary PCs: no GPU, and no data leaves the workstation.

I treated the clinical number, not the detector score, as the thing to get right, and replaced every assumption of the first version by a measurement.

What the audit of version 1 found

  • 195 s of CPU time for a one-second clip: unusable on a laboratory PC.
  • A head-shape gate taken from anatomy (aspect ratio 1.4 to 2.0) contained only 29.7% of annotated heads. At 10 px, a head's aspect ratio is mostly pixel quantisation. Applied to version 1's detections, the gate cut recall from 0.593 to 0.074.
  • The 300-detection cap was hit in 33.3% of frames, so dense samples silently lost cells.
  • Heads are bright on a dark field (median intensity 190 against 33 for the background), so inverting the colours only made the input less natural for the detector.

04

SpermNet, the detector

The detector gains came from geometry, not from capacity. A median head spans 4.8 feature cells at stride 2 but 1.2 at stride 8, the finest level of a YOLO11 detection head, and 0.3 at stride 32. So I designed a small network that keeps resolution instead of a deep one that throws it away.

Extent of a median head in feature cells, by output stride

Stride 2 (SpermNet)4.84 cells
Stride 42.42
Stride 8 (YOLO11 P3)1.21
Stride 16 (P4)0.60
Stride 32 (P5)0.30

A detector can only localise a target that spans about one feature cell. Only the finest level of a standard pyramid carries signal for 10 px heads.

  • Size: 375,621 parameters, 53 times fewer than YOLO11m (20.05 M), in a 1.5 MB file.
  • Training: focal-type loss on the heatmap and L1 on size and offset, about 17 minutes per model on two CPU cores.
  • Output: decoding applies 3×3 peak suppression, adds the sub-pixel offset and reads the size at the peak. Sub-pixel centroids matter because velocity differentiates position noise.
  • Tracking: detections are linked online. Each track predicts its next position from its recent motion, and detections are assigned inside a distance gate by linear assignment. No appearance network.

05

Scale, kinematics and grading

Velocities in µm/s depend on the scale in micrometres per pixel. The engine takes it from a stage-micrometer calibration measured in the console, or from a value the laboratory enters; for this microscope the laboratory's calibration is 0.827 µm/px. Only when neither exists does it estimate the scale from the specimen, from the median detected head length taken as 9.23 µm (the detector's box includes the phase halo).

That estimate is a last resort. Head size differs between men (median head area per recording from 63.9 to 103.1 px²), so even with its best reference length it leaves a spread of 7.1% RMS between recordings, 18% at worst. Version 1 used a textbook 4.7 µm head length, which put the scale at about half its calibrated value. Under an estimated scale the engine still reports motility, but withholds the a/b split, which depends on an absolute speed boundary.

Conventions, stated and measured

The parameters are the standard ones (VCL, VSL, VAP, LIN, STR, WOB, ALH, BCF), but their values depend on conventions that CASA papers rarely state. I fixed each one explicitly, then measured what it is worth by re-measuring the 45 recordings from the same tracks with that one convention changed.

  • Average path: a three-point running mean, truncated at the ends. ALH is the largest distance between the path and the average path.
  • Jitter dead band: a position moves only when the detection leaves a 0.3 px circle. A static head's sub-pixel centre otherwise wanders enough to read as a few µm/s of VCL.
  • Minimum track: 10 points. Shorter fragments, 24% of all tracker tracks, are dropped, and gaps inside a track are interpolated so a missed frame does not shorten the path.

Grading

Each track is graded once, from its kinematics over the whole recording, so a cell that pauses keeps one grade. Every boundary is on VCL. PR = a + b, NP = c and IM = d, following the three classes of the WHO manual (6th ed.) and the a to d grades of earlier editions. The rapid boundary follows the stage temperature.

The error that costs the clinician

For an F1 difference of 0.08, the error on the number a clinician reads differs sevenfold. A detector gap that looks modest in F1 is not modest clinically, which is why I rank errors by their effect on the endpoint.

Bias on the motile fraction at matched recall 0.80 (points)

SpermNet: 382 false positives, 33.2% moving−1.6
YOLO11m: 705 false positives, 20.0% moving−11.1

Both detectors miss almost the same number of cells (268 and 266). The difference is the false positives that do not move, each counted as an immotile cell.

Where it stops working

On the five public clips of the SVIA dataset, recorded in bright field with a 20× objective, the unchanged detector found 49% of the annotated heads at a precision of 27%, even after inverting the contrast and resizing the frames to match. SpermNet has learned one microscope's view of a head, not a head in general: other optics need retraining.

07

The Andrology Console

The engine ships inside a desktop application so a laboratory can use it on its own PC, with records kept on that workstation.

  • ONNX with verified parity: on real recordings the ONNX export gives identical detections in every frame, with a maximum centre difference of 6.1 × 10⁻⁵ px. Peak suppression and decoding are re-implemented in NumPy.
  • A smaller installer: dropping PyTorch (1.2 GB on disk to run a 1.5 MB model) took the installer from about 1.7 GB to 450 MB.
  • Refuse rather than guess: every analysis is stamped with the thresholds and scale it was graded under; quantities that depend on an unknown scale are withheld, and the application never updates itself, so a measurement cannot change between samples without a decision.
  • An overlay that matches the numbers: the review video draws only the tracks the result counts. Tracks and head positions are stored per field, so a field can be excluded, or a sample restated under a new calibration, without re-running the detector, and the overlay is redrawn in the new grades.
  • Security: roles and sessions, a hash-chained audit log, and three rounds of security review (find, fix, re-attack the fixes), each leaving regression tests.
  • Workflow: patients, collection diary, worklist, upload and queue, overlay review against the original video, calibration management, graded results, reports in PDF, JSON, CSV and Excel, in English and French.

08

Limits and what comes next

  • Labels come from one annotator over a small area (1,349 heads in 48 patches, one 320×320 tracking window).
  • The 45 recordings come from one laboratory and one microscope, and each lasts one second. Field-to-field agreement measures precision, not accuracy: a bias shared by every field would not show in it.
  • The YOLO baselines were not retrained per fold under the same budget.
  • At 25 fps ALH and BCF are under-sampled and are best read as relative measures.
  • The detector does not transfer to bright-field optics. Retraining on several microscopes, external validation on phase-contrast data from other laboratories and a second annotator are still to do. The system is not presented as clinically validated.

The current version is written up as a single-author paper, SpermNet: CPU-only detection, tracking and kinematic grading of human sperm motility in phase-contrast video (PDF, 6 pages).
