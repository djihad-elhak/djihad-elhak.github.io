  1. Sept. 2025 – Mar. 20267 months

    Research intern, computer vision and AI for medical imaging

    CERIST, Data Science Unit, Algeria

    Automated sperm motility analysis for a reproductive biology laboratory: find every spermatozoon in microscope video (heads about 10 pixels wide, up to 218 per frame at 25 fps), follow it, measure its velocity and grade it as the WHO manual does, on an ordinary PC with no GPU. Full write-up: SpermNet case study.

    Audit of the first version

    • Built the baseline (YOLO11m detection, DeepSORT tracking with a MobileNetV2 re-ID network) and measured where it failed: 195 s of CPU per one-second clip, merged touching heads, tracks broken after 15.4 of 25 frames on average.
    • Showed that a head-shape gate cut recall from 0.593 to 0.074 and that the 300-detection cap was hit in a third of frames, and removed both.

    Data and evaluation

    • Curated 15 one-second recordings (5 men × 3 motility strata) by reviewing raw acquisitions frame by frame in OpenCV and re-encoding them losslessly; 2,410 labelled objects for detection and tracking.
    • Set up five-fold cross-validation grouped by video, tracking metrics (MOTA, IDF1, HOTA) and an analysis of how each detector error biases the motile fraction a clinician reads.

    Model and tracking

    • Designed and trained SpermNet, a 375,621-parameter U-shaped detector with a stride-2 centre-point output and a three-channel spatio-temporal input: F1 0.769 against 0.690 for a tuned YOLO11m, with 53 times fewer parameters.
    • Replaced DeepSORT with a motion-gated tracker and showed association was not the bottleneck: IDF1 0.724 against 0.621 with the same tracker, and 10.4 s per clip on two CPU cores instead of 195 s.

    Measurement

    • Ran a stage-micrometer test that showed the scale estimated from head length was off by 38.7%; replaced it with optical-train calibration and a scale-free grading criterion that reproduces the three-class report within 1.5 points.
    • Computed kinematics (VCL, VSL, VAP, LIN, STR, WOB) from sub-pixel centroids and graded each track a to d, with the rapid boundary following stage temperature.

    Product and deployment

    • Exported the detector to ONNX with verified numerical parity and cut the installer from 1.7 GB to 450 MB by removing PyTorch.
    • Built the Andrology Console: Electron and React/TypeScript over a FastAPI engine, with patients, collection diary, worklist, overlay review against the video, calibration, laboratory parameters, PDF/CSV/Excel reports in English and French, roles, a hash-chained audit trail and three rounds of security review.
    • Compared the results with the SCA reference system on 15 samples from 5 patients at 3 motility levels, and wrote the work up as a single-author preprint.
